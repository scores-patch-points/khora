# What is "good enough" for a language?

*Standing finding, 2026-10-09, asked after the German seam's first run.
One sentence: a seam is good enough when its EO clauses are **native-grammar
true**, its **cast is the text's beings not its vocabulary**, and its claims
survive **the byte**, at rates a falsifier would accept — measured, not
cheered.*

## The four gates (every language)

A language is READ, not knitted, when all four are verifiably bound:

1. **EO-native clauses.** The seam emits the cube's clause shape
   `{verb, subject, object, dative}` with role-seat cells, by the language's
   own grammar: Greek by case-ending, Sanskrit by IAST ending, English by
   word-order position, German by article-declared case. Not "good enough to
   try" — good enough only when the case/cell is true for a *foreign* native
   sentence you read by hand.

2. **The cast is beings, not vocabulary.** This is the gate German just
   FAILED. German capitalizes all nouns, so "capital + recur + class-null"
   floods the cast with `Erde / Kräfte / Tränen`. A being is *who does / is
   done to*, discoverable only by **what appears in the seat across scenes** —
   not by orthography. English names pass because English capitalizes names;
   German names drown because German capitalizes nouns. A seam that returns
   the vocabulary instead of the beings is a dictionary, not a reader.

3. **Claims survive the byte.** `accuracy.mjs` (DMD-bounded, visit the
   address, never verbatim) must confirm a hand-written proposition about the
   text — on the German, on the Greek, on the English — at both standard and
   lowercased. A claim a native reader knows is true must be attested; a
   claim invented must be refused.

4. **The falsifier across genres within the language.** One language is many
   genres. German prose (Frege) ≠ German drama (Faust's `FAUST.` speaker
   labels, the vocative address "Wo bist du, Faust?"). The seam must read
   both — the genre norm parameterizes *which signal carries the seat*
   (dialogue quotient, vocative, stage apparatus), never *whether structure
   exists*.

## The bar, stated to be refused

- **Cast:** the text's named beings (Faust, Gretchen) must bind in the seat
  at least as often as they occur as subjects, and the top-10 cast must not
  be common nouns.
- **EO truth:** a judge's hand-read of 10 native sentences must agree with
  the seam's (case, cell, subject/object/dative) on ≥8/10.
- **Byte:** ≥8/10 true claims attested; every invented claim refused.
- **Genre:** at least two texts of different register must both read.

## German now

The native grammar (article-case) is real and holds — Frege reads at 38%
object binding, the Dat/Acc split is genuine. But the seam is **not yet good
enough**: drama apparatus is unbound, the vocative kills a would-be subject,
and the vocabulary-flood fails gate 2. The German cast will be the beings only
when `isCaseFreeBeing` stops trusting orthography and starts trusting the
**seat across scenes** — the same lesson English taught, from the other side.

*A seam that returns the vocabulary instead of the beings is a dictionary,
not a reader. Good enough is measured: native-true clauses, beings not nouns,
claims that survive the byte, and two registers read.*
