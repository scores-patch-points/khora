# The fold chat: ideation as a linted process, and the doors

2026-11-23. Three things the owner asked for, in their order: "the ideation being a janus linted process that we can see in the fold chat"; "as an app all the repos write to pharos"; "doors and explicit tool calling with slash commands, but it should be willing and able to do this research itself", then "a signal for doing research IS not knowing an answer".

## Who writes what

One log, `~/.pharos/fold-demo` (`CHAT_PHAROS_DIR` to change it), one file per writer inside it. The ruling is janus's own (`pharos-door.mjs`, 2026-11-14): no repo reports to another; each writes its own entries and reads the others'.

| writer | what it writes | through |
|---|---|---|
| `khora-chat` | statements, the cast, frames, the nine-operator turn (void, thought, candidate, verdict, utterance), **candidate** entries (every thing a reply could say), **assessment** entries for what khora's own organs computed, **tool** entries (every door call) | `record.mjs`, `ideation.mjs`, `doors.mjs` |
| `janus` | **assessment** entries for what janus's organs tested (a person is not their own wife; no giver states a spouse) | janus's own door (`janus-door.mjs` mounts it; the chat does not write janus's verdicts for it) |

The standing of each candidate is not remembered by the chat: it is read back off the log (every writer's entries, in log order) and projected by janus's reasoning spiral (`reasoning-spiral.js`). A candidate with no ground is `unlicensed`; a deduction with no named license is refused and stays on the record; a claim whose premise is refuted or unmet is `suspended`; a giver's word is `licensed`, never `supported`. A janus checkout that is not there is a typed gap that says how to get it (`JANUS_HOME`, or clone beside khora), not a silent degrade.

## What a reply does

1. The question is read; what it could be answered with is **put forward as candidates** (`ideation.propose`), each with its ground (spans of the thread, or a named giver).
2. Each is **tested by an instrument** and the verdict is written by the instrument's owner. Example, "who was his wife?" after "who is Tolstoy?":
   - "his" stands for Tolstoy: khora's cast, witnessed by the span where the referent was introduced → holds.
   - Tolstoy is Tolstoy's wife (what the old identity route answered): janus `kinship.makeFact` refuses it (`self_relation`) → ruled out.
   - Tolstoy has a wife the thread names: janus's rule for "wife" (read from the Wiktionary gloss) over the facts the thread holds → open (`no_spouse_fact`).
   - Sophia Tolstaya: the door `relation` (Wikidata P26), a giver's record → rests on a giver's word.
3. The reply is the surviving claim, plain, with its sources as numbered citations (no "from Wikidata" in the sentence); "How I thought" shows every candidate, who tested it and who wrote the verdict.

## Doors

`doors.mjs`. A door is declared (name, what it is for, usage, what it needs, whether it is on and why not), read-only, and answers with its source. `/doors` lists them; `/name args` calls one by hand; every call is a `tool` entry in the log.

Research is triggered by **not knowing**. A question the thread cannot answer ends in a typed gap, a gap has a shape (`no_spouse_fact`, `unknown_referent`, `no_answer`), and a door declares the shapes it fills (`fills`). The chat matches shape to door and goes to look without being asked (never by a keyword of the question). What a door brings back enters the thread as a **given** sentence (said by the giver, not the user), so questions can be answered from it with the sentence quoted back under the giver's name. "Go research it" does nothing the chat was not already built to do: with a gap open it looks again at that gap; with none it says it is missing nothing.

An imperative is acted on when it is about what is not known: its object is a pronoun or absent (the open gap), or a name the thread has not met. An imperative about the world ("open the door") is refused and points at `/doors`.

## The page, and what it does while it works

The page is a chat app, not a command line (the owner, 2026-11-23: "make it all feel more like a chat app, the slash commands aren't great"): replies are plain text with numbered **source chips** (`[1] Wikidata · Q7243`), one collapsed **Reasoning** (what was used, what was considered, who tested it), a typing line that says what is being done while a door works ("Searching Wikipedia for …", streamed as lines by `/api/chat` when the page asks), Copy and Try again on a reply, New chat, and a persona picker. Slash commands still work for anyone who types them, and are not advertised.

## Personas: a prompt read as a document about the assistant

A persona is not a mood. `personas.mjs` reads a published system prompt through the reader: the **protagonist** is the name (or "you") the prompt most often makes the subject of a clause, counted over sentences spread across the whole prompt; the **identity** is the clause that says what it is (found by its copula wherever the parser hangs it: "This iteration of Claude is Claude Sonnet 5.5" gives "Claude Sonnet 5.5"; availability like "is accessible via" does not count); the prompt's sentences about the protagonist are read into a **fold of their own** (door: given, the prompt's words). A question about "you" is asked of that fold with "you" turned into the name: "who are you?" is the identity; anything else is answered from the prompt's sentences with them cited, or says "That isn't something my instructions say." The picker lists only prompts (not skills, agent files, tool docs), labelled by file and date, because "claude" used to mean Claude Code's 2 KB FleetView agent file.

## Research by hunt (door `hunt`), then by search

For a gap of shape `no_answer` the chat first tries the **hunt** (`native/organs/void-hunt.js` and `hunt-sources.js`, another session's organs, imported dynamically: absent, the door says it is off): the question declares its void (what kind of thing is missing, which names are already taken, the relation), whole Wikipedia pages are read for the sentences that fill it, every candidate is listed with its page, section and sentence, and an answer is named only if one candidate stands alone. The reply cites the page and the sentence (a numbered chip), and janus calls the claim a giver's word. A last look (`hunt-check.mjs`, the Brydon test: see THE-MAN-IN-THE-BOX.md) refuses to state an answer that another candidate out-supports in pages and lists both instead. If the hunt cannot take the question at all (no name to anchor on) the plain search below runs; if it can and finds nothing, or throttled, it says so and nothing else is tried. First live runs (EXPLORATORY, 2026-11-23, real Wikipedia): before the organ was repaired "Who wrote War and Peace?" gave "Suetonius" (one sentence about the title's history) and "Who did Mel Gibson play in Lethal Weapon?" ran past 150 s; the organ's owner fixed the joined title, the by-line ("a novel by X") and added a 60 s budget, and then "Who wrote War and Peace?" gave "Leo Tolstoy." (7 s), "Who did Mel Gibson play in Lethal Weapon?" gave "Martin Riggs." (4 s, cited to the film's Cast section and the actor's page), "Who founded Microsoft?" gave "Bill Gates and Paul Allen." (2 s), and "Who composed The Magic Flute?" was not answered (56 s, "Wikipedia has Prince Tamino, K and Schikaneder next to those names, but no sentence says how they're related"): a decline, correctly. The last look only weighs candidates the relation attests, never a name merely seen nearby.

## Research by search

A gap of shape `no_answer` goes to the `search` door: the question's content words (chosen by part of speech, never a word list, so lowercase names do not matter) are searched on Wikipedia, the best two matches are read into the thread as Wikipedia's words, and the question is asked again. If what was read does not answer it, the reply says what was read. The door client caches for ten minutes and waits out HTTP 429 and 5xx twice, honouring Retry-After. The `person` door judges its first hit: every word of the name must be in the label, else the closest is named (a search for "marie curie" must not return "Marie de France").

## What the chat thinks a message is: claims, not a router (the owner, 2026-11-23)

"We can't just have this emerging as the middle tier of structure. The thing has to be defining what it thinks is going on, and those assertions are revisable." Until now `turn()` decided in passing (a word table, the question mark, "has it got a verb") and replied as if certain: "Sorry dear", "Yes." and a Chinese sentence all got "Okay.". `acts.mjs` makes the decision a set of **claims about the message**, put forward as candidates like any other (so janus's spiral projects their standing and the page shows them under "What I considered"):

- Each claim ("this message counts as a question") **rests on marks**: a final question mark, an auxiliary before the subject, an imperative form, no predicate at all, a subject with a predicate, a small-talk word, numbers and an operator, a slash, a list of things and people taking turns. A mark may also **oppose** a claim (a question mark opposes "a statement": "Pierre loves Natasha?" is a question and the statement reading is shown as ruled out, with the mark that rules it out).
- **One declared rule** judges them: an act holds when a mark supports it and none opposes it; a specific act that holds (a command, a puzzle, arithmetic, statements then a question) is the working reading alone; if two acts hold, the message is **ambiguous**: both stay open, the chat says so ("I took that as a social remark; it could also be a question. Say “no” if I got it wrong.") and routes to the act that changes the thread least if wrong (social, then question, then request, then statement; declared with that reason); if none holds the message is **unread**, a typed gap, never a guess.
- The routing in `turn()` follows the standing act and nothing else. The word tables that remain (small talk, question words) are inputs the marks rest on, never decisions.
- **Revision.** A message that opens with the reader's own negative-polarity interjection ("no, …") is a claim that it corrects the last message that said something. It is judged like any other, and if it holds the earlier reading is **withdrawn**: the spiral shows it as withdrawn, what was built on it is suspended, and its sentences are no longer answers. A social remark in between does not shift the target ("really?" does not take "Pierre loves Natasha." back). With nothing to correct, the claim is ruled out and "no" is only a remark.
- What an answer rests on: an answer built from the thread (a candidate from the thread, a derivation) depends on the message having been read as it was, so withdrawing the reading suspends it. A giver's word does not (it stays "rests on a giver's word").

Measured on the real-chat sample (EXPLORATORY, 3,600 messages, `eval/chat-register`): social replies 31.5% of replies, replies that are a statement stored as a fact 41.6% (84.4% at the start), messages it could not read at all 8.4% (the Chinese sentence and punctuation-only lines now say "I couldn't tell what kind of message that was" instead of "Okay."). Still open: telling an apology from an acknowledgement (no giver-backed route: `eval/chat-register/social-acts`), a multi-sentence noisy message with no clear act is "unread" rather than stored as junk, and the reply for a language with no codec waits for the reader's typed gap.

## What leaves the machine

Automatic research sends only words the reader knows. A name, a number, or a word the reader's part-of-speech prior holds can go to Wikipedia as a search; a message with any other content word (romanised Tamil, a misspelling like "captial", text in another script) is not researched, and the reply says so ("I couldn't read some of those words, so I didn't look anything up."). Found by the other session's census: a romanised-Tamil question was being sent to Wikipedia as its query. This is a privacy line and a quality line at once; the proper gap for a language with no codec is the reader's (mechanic 5), and when it exists it replaces this check. A word the reader tags as a name passes, so an unknown word with a capital still goes out as a name: a limit of the check.

## Two valves removed by the Brydon test (the other session's measurement, 2026-11-23)

The other session ran 390 real messages (130 per source) through a fresh thread each, network stubbed and counted, and compared what the act layer DECLARED with what the chat DID: 38 of 390 (9.7%) sent words out, 29 of them one-word messages declared social ("Der", "Gn", "Come", "Okie", "Bangalore") that the chat then took for bare names and looked up; of 618 messages declared social on "no predicate" alone, only 10% were interjection-only by the reader's own marks (the rest were names, bot output, commands, answers to someone, questions). Both are valves by the test in THE-MAN-IN-THE-BOX.md:

- **A remark is social only on positive evidence**: it opens with an interjection (the reader's own tag) and has no predicate, or it is a known greeting or thanks. A bare fragment is not social by having nothing else in it: it is "something I can't place", and the reply says so ("I'm not sure what you mean by “Bangalore”."). Nothing is stored and nothing is sent. A man says "Okay." to an acknowledgement, not to a name or a command.
- **Capitalisation is not evidence of a name.** A bare name is answered from the notebook if the thread holds that person; otherwise it is not looked up. Evidence of a name is the notebook or a question about it ("Who is Tolstoy?"), not a capital letter.
- **A pronoun with nothing to point to** ("is it worth doing?") is asked about ("I don't know what “it” stands for") and the rest is not researched around.

Measured by `eval/chat-register/declared-vs-done.mjs` (EXPLORATORY, one run, a sample the other session did not hand-label): declared and executed disagree in 34 of 390 before, 0 after; messages that sent words out 38 (9.7%) before, 5 (1.3%) after the first two changes, 3 (0.8%) after the pronoun rule: a request whose object token is garbage ("Check mail.i …", still looked up because the reader tags it a name), "What? Haha help what?", and "could that be the problem" (the reader does not tag "that" as a pronoun there). "Sorry dear" and "good morning" are now "I'm not sure what you mean": a person would know them, and we have no giver-backed way to (`eval/chat-register/social-acts`), which is a gap said, not an "Okay." pretended.

## The chat asks back, and the next fragment is the answer (mechanic 7, first slice)

A man who cannot tell what "it" stands for asks, and uses the answer. "Is it worth doing?" with nothing to point to now ends with "Who or what do you mean?", and the chat remembers that it asked. If the next message is a fragment ("the course"), it is read as the answer: a claim "this message answers your question about “it”" (supported, on a fragment straight after the question), the original question is asked again with the answer in its place ("is the course worth doing?"), and the reply says so in its trace. Any message that is not a fragment drops the open question (he moves on), and a fragment with nothing asked is unchanged ("I'm not sure what you mean by …"). **The answer must fit what was asked** (the other session's probe found 5 of 6 non-answers paired: "did he arrive?" then "the course" became "did the course arrive?", and "Hahaha" and "!pt" went out in a search): the gap is a pronoun with a gender and number, and the answer must be a noun phrase of the right kind, read from the reader's marks. For he or she it must be a name, whose lean in the given-name table must not contradict the pronoun (no lean is no contradiction); for it it must be a noun the reader knows, or a name the thread already holds. Otherwise the chat says why it can't use it ("“he” is a person and “the course” is not a name"), asks again, keeps the question open, records the refused pairing as a ruled-out claim, and sends nothing; the rebuilt question of a good pairing goes through the same guards as any question. **A prior that was missing, said.** The other session found that `loadNameGender()` returns null in this checkout (`native/adapters/text/name-gender/eng.json`, a giver table of given names, is not here or in its history; only `corpus-en.json`, counts from four novels, is, and it is a thin table: "mina" f 10, m 8, "natasha" absent), so every gender lean in the chat, the cast's and this fit check's, had been running with no table and saying nothing. The chat now says so: `state().priors.nameGender` carries the gap and the reason, and a pairing of a name to "he" or "she" records in its claim that it could not check the name. Whether the chat should use the canonical corpus prior (`gender-from-corpus.js`) or the giver file should be built is the reading owner's call; four novels, one of them the book the chat was developed against, is not a table to lean on. Only the dangling-pronoun question is built; asking back about an ambiguous act ("did you mean that as a question?"), which would replace the least-harm routing valve, and yes/no answers to a question the chat asked (12 of the 27 placeable real fragments, by the other session's reading) are next on this mechanism.

## Local before outside, whole names, doubt on one mark (the other session's probe, 2026-11-23)

- **The guard is for doors that leave the machine.** "What does serendipity mean?" is a rare word, which is what a definition is for, and the dictionary is on this machine: the question is routed to the local `define` door and nothing is sent. The unreadable-words check applies only to the automatic research that goes to Wikipedia.
- **The thing asked about is the whole name.** "Who is Marie Curie?" looks up "marie curie" (head plus its compound dependents) and says "I haven't been told about Marie Curie", not "Marie". Lowercase names are still read as ordinary words by the reader (mechanic 3, the parsing session's), so "who is marie curie?" is not understood as a name yet.
- **A question that rests on the final “?” alone is fragile on purpose.** Measured by the other session on 600 real messages per source (EXPLORATORY): the working act survives losing the mark 61% of the time when "?" is its only support, against 95-98% when a question word, an auxiliary before the subject or the clause's shape backs it too. The chat now says so ("I took that as a question because of the question mark. Say “no” if you meant it as something you're telling me.") instead of treating it as settled.

## Negation, and yes/no questions (mechanic 8, 2026-11-23)

Found by probing the chat: "Mina did not give Jonathan a book." was stored as the frame "Mina gave Jonathan a book", and "Who gave Jonathan a book?" answered "Mina." The engine's frames now carry the clause's **polarity mark**, the reader's own `Polarity=Neg` on a dependent of the verb (not a word list; the fork session's finding for 22 languages is that negation is no more subordinate than affirmation, so the mark is per clause). With `polarity: true` (the thread asks this way; the book panel keeps its old answers) a frame answers only a question of its own polarity, and an affirmed frame is **retracted** by a later negated frame about the same event. The reply to "Who gave Jonathan a book?" after the negated statement is "Not Mina: you told me “Mina did not give Jonathan a book.”"; a yes/no question gets "Yes: you told me …" or "No: you told me …" with the sentence; a failed lookup is said as a failed lookup. Not covered: the reader marks `Polarity=Neg` only on "not" and "n't" (not on "never", "nobody", "no"), which is the reader's to fix, and a correction that replaces an object ("no, it was a pen") is not read as a replacement.

## Addressing, and questions about the thread's own people

A leading name and comma before a question or a request ("Fold, who is Marie Curie?") is who is spoken to, a deixis and not a referent: it is taken off before routing (structure only: the first row names, the second is a comma, the rest is itself a question or an imperative), so nothing researches "Fold" and a list that begins with a name ("Pierre, Natasha and Mina went home.") is still a statement. A question whose every name is a referent the thread already holds ("Does Natasha love Pierre?" after "Pierre loves Natasha.") is not researched: the world cannot say what was said in the thread, so the reply is "I haven't been told that." (trace step `no_research`).

## What is not built (open, with where to look)

- **Answering "who did X play in Y"** from what it read: the opening says "starring Mel Gibson and Danny Glover as … Martin Riggs and Roger Murtaugh", and pairing the two lists is a role-level equivalence the engine does not have. The research now fires and reads the right articles; the answer needs the pairing.
- **Yes/no questions** ("did Mina give Jonathan a book?") return an empty sentence; follow-ups ("where was she born?") are not carried. A fragment with no predicate ("good morning", "Ok dear") is now a social act and is not stored; a bare name is a topic; a question about "you" or "me" is not researched. The owner's census of 53 asks (fork session, exploratory) puts it at 13 right, 24 handled, 27 gaps, 11 rate-limited.


- **Kin facts from the thread.** `kinFacts()` in `thread.mjs` is empty: "Sophia was Tolstoy's wife" is not turned into a janus fact (janus `scripts/kinship/bind-kin.mjs` reads kin from text). Until then the thread-side answer to a kin question is always a gap, and the doors fill it.
- **Puzzles** (door `reason`, on when `native/organs/puzzle-reader.js` is present; that organ and its test are the fork session's, uncommitted at the time of writing). A message that holds a list of things, at least two people and turns of who said what is read by the puzzle reader into the epistemic reasoner (`native/organs/epistemic-core.js`). The reader LISTS the readings of the text (who holds what, what "yes" means), each is run, and each is put forward as a candidate: the board puzzle gives six, one holds (different letters of the word, "yes" means they know), three are refuted, two are open, and the answer "dog" rests on the reading that holds, so janus's spiral would suspend it if that reading fell. The reply says the answer and how the possible words shrank (5, 3, 1). Cheryl's birthday is not solved: the reader says what it read (10 dates, 2 people) and what it did not (what each holds, "I know that Bernard does not know"), and runs nothing smaller.
- **Research quality.** `Who wrote War and Peace?` after reading Wikipedia's opening is not answered (the opening says "best known for the novels", a different frame), and the topic the chat picks for its own research is the first name in the question. Pinned.
- **The door names and the verbs of requests are English** (the 206 text-field reads counted in HOW-WE-ITERATE are still there; this adds a few). The door-fill matching itself is by gap shape.
- **janus cannot take a giver's record as a kin fact** (it requires a span of text), so a researched answer stands as `licensed` and is never counted as janus's evidence. That is janus's law, kept.
