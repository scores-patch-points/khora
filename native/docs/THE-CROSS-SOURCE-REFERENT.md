# The cross-source referent

*Identity at a point, across languages — a standing rule of the reading, canonized 2026-10-11 from a measured run.*

**Standing: finding.** The machine that decides "are these two mentions the same being" — `kernel/identity-induction.js`,
the four null tests of `identity-at-a-point.md` — refuses to bind a being across two translations of the same
instrument. The refusal is structural, measured, and the reason it is structural is the one seat that is missing.

## The rule

**A being crosses a language only at its induced-kinds dependency position, never at its relations' words.**

Three ways to get cross-source identity wrong, each now refused by the record:

1. **by spelling** — a surface→surface table. Refused: "a referent is not a spelling or a surface-run"
   (`identity-at-a-point.md` epithet corollary); recurrence/co-occurrence is not identity (`kernel/identity-exclusion.js`).
2. **by meet over self-supports** — declaring pairs and letting the for-whom meet hold them. Refused by its own gate:
   a self-supplied frame is an empty frame (measured relevance 0.012 → 0.083; the DMD coherence of a self-pair stream
   sits below its shuffled null).
3. **by relation-labels across languages** — the four induction tests on the relation's own words. Refused on
   UDHR (2026-10-11): English `law` ↔ Latin `ius`, English `law` ↔ Spanish `ley` both stop at
   `unbound · no_positive_evidence`, because `rel:entitled` vs `rel:habet` are **disjoint features** — the lift vectors
   have nothing shared to align (obs 0.000 vs the null's ceiling). Same-form, same-language pairs align; cross-language
   pairs cannot, because the words differ even when the being does not.

The negatives the same run refuted prove the machine is not broken: `ley↔desarrollo` is `contradicted (worlds_differ,
floor 0.500)`; `law↔religion` and `law↔justícia` are `unbound` — none of the wrong pairs binds.

## Why the bridged path is the right one

Identity is the fold at a point **following the induced-kinds dependency order** (seat 2 of `identity-at-a-point.md`).
For universes to align across a translation they must align on what a being IS — its position in the shared
kind-dependency graph — not on the verb that happened to sit beside it in one language. That seat's precondition
(meaning relations, then kinds over them) is exactly the pillar whose corpus-scale run stands **FALSIFIED**
(`eval/identity/corpus-kinds-RESULTS.md`, 2026-10-07: the reader's relations are function words, and the inducer is
O(n²)). So the honest chain is: **meaning relations → induced kinds → this judge, at the kind position.** Nothing
below that earns a cross-source referent.

## Falsifying controls

- A cross-source positive that binds while the sides' direct relation features remain disjoint falsifies rule 3: the
  alignment is possible in words after all.
- A negative that binds after the read improves (any of `law↔religion`, `ley↔desarrollo`, `law↔justícia`) falsifies
  the discriminant.
- A `law↔ius` that STILL fails at the induced-kind position, once kinds are earned, moves the missing seat elsewhere.
