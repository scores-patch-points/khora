## What a description wormhole is
"the girl", "the old man": all places where the description occurs are linked to one cast referent, only if no difference survives (the share of bound occurrences going to one referent beats random presence-binding by z >= sqrt(2 ln H); else a typed gap).
## Results
- Links admitted: about 2 per book on the 34 read books (74 in all); 6 in the 8 test books. About 90% of description heads occur fewer than 3 times bound ("the door", "the room"): they are not characters, and are typed gaps.
- W1 (identity test beats unfiltered binding on the silver set of appositives): read books 4/7 admitted links right vs 6/17 for unfiltered binding; test books 1/2 vs 1/2. The direction is right; the sample is too small to say anything. NOT TESTABLE as preregistered.
- W2 (no-name questions gain an entry and reach the answer): development 8 of 94 gained an entry (gold in the cut 3/8 vs BM25 1/8); TEST 0 of 30. NOT SUPPORTED. The prediction of 15-35% entries was wrong.
- W3 (mouths raise gold-in-cut for named questions): development 0.461 -> 0.468 [0.000, 0.030]; test 0.342 -> 0.342 [0.000, 0.000]. NOT SUPPORTED.
## What it says
Description wormholes are almost always too rare to matter in these books. The cast is already the main wormhole set (all mentions of a character are linked), and the measured gap is the questions that name no character (26% of the earlier questions; 30 of 68 here). Descriptions do not close it. The places a no-name question could enter by are other wormholes: the question's wording matched to the text's (paraphrase), places and things, and kinds.
Not tested: those three; answer F1. The short books also have fewer questions per book and are mostly children's tales and short stories.
