- The GUM-measured gender table changed no binding (179 gendered entities, 468 words: too thin).
- NOTE-2/3 (givers): the Wikidata given-name snapshot (33,817 male, 20,526 female, 3,763 unisex labels; the male and female streams were TRUNCATED by the query service, common names measured present) is a giver with provenance. Reliability against the gender the text uses (training slices): r = 0.854 (140 of 164 beings; covers 46% of gendered beings); one clause of in-text evidence q = 0.862 (25 of 29 beings; small).
- The giver's gender prior as a hard prior lost right bindings (306/28 vs 321/30). The surprise-weighted gender (NOTE-4: log-odds from r and q, activation weighted by P(match)) also did (306/28, coverage 60.5%): 15 of the 17 bindings it dropped belong to one cast being, "Alex Wilson Betty", two people merged into one being (the giver says m from Alex, the text says she of Betty): a conflict between the dictionary and use inside one being, a false merge. Neither is adopted. On the toy texts the surprise-weighted gender stops he binding Mary, Anna, Helen (21 bindings fall to 6), which is the case it is for.
- Root cause found on the toys: the rung's clause-local gender evidence rule takes the one named being in a clause as the referent of a gendered pronoun in it ("then he called Mary" -> Mary is male) because the real referent (a sentence-initial-only name) was never admitted. Open.
