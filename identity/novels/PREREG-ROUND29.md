# PREREG-ROUND29 — compute the nesting from the read, on the fly, like a person; structured data only scores

You said: we should not rely on structured data being available; the holograph's own relationships should let us know this type of dependency; compute it on the fly like a person does. So rounds 27 and 28 (judge over a Wikidata nest) become the reference side only. Here the nest is read from the text, and Wikidata is the scorer.

## What is built: `native/adapters/text/nest-from-read.js`
Input: the entry a read returns (`cast`, `edges`, `sentences`). No lookup, no list of places, no class table.
1. PLACE-LIKE, computed from the text: a being is place-like if it is the far end of a locative edge (the edge label ends in in, at, to, from, near, into or within: "was born in", "moved to", "in") or if it sits on a containment edge with a place-like being; closed under the containment edges until nothing changes. A person named in a comma list is never reached, because nothing locative touches it.
2. CONTAINMENT CUES (declared grammar, end1 inside end2): a bare comma between two place-like beings ("Topeka, Kansas"), "in", "of", "at", "within", "inside", and the appositive heads "capital of", "seat of", "part of", "city of", "town of", "county of", "state of", "province of", "region of", "village of", "suburb of", "borough of" (articles and commas around a label are stripped).
3. EVIDENCE: each cue edge is a vote for (child, parent); the same pair repeated across the text adds up. If both directions are voted, the larger count wins; on a tie the being that recurs with more distinct children is the container (a container recurs with many children, a child keeps one container).
4. PARENT: each child takes its most-voted parent; a second parent with votes is recorded as contested. Chains follow parents (cycle guard). A being with no vote has no parent: a typed gap (the text is silent), never a guess.
5. The judge over an extracted nest: grain by position, declared: city, town, village, municipality = the subject's own place (index 0); country, nation = the root-most holon; any other noun (state, province, county...) = the middle holon when the chain has exactly three; otherwise gap. "Where" with an asker: the asker's own place is read from the same text if it names one; its path's shared ground with the subject's decides what counts, as in round 28. Everything else is round 28's judge logic.

## Text and gold
- Text: the English Wikipedia lead paragraph of each person in the round 28 giver data (actors, birthplace known), fetched with the title from the Wikidata sitelink (`identity/place-grain/fetch-leads.py`; hash in the freeze file). Zero-download development text on disk (LitBank, GUM) is used only to debug the cue extraction by eye.
- Gold: the round 28 Wikidata nest of the person's birthplace (parent edges, transitive). Label matching is by case-folded string; a label that does not resolve is unscored and counted.
- Split by hash of the Wikidata id: even = development (debug only), odd = held-out, scored once.

## Claims, decided before running
- E1 REACH (reported): the share of leads whose text names the birthplace as a being in the cast.
- E2 PRECISION: of the (child, parent) steps on the extracted chain from the birthplace, the share where the parent is a Wikidata ancestor of the child. Direction errors (the parent is the child's descendant) counted separately.
- E3 RECALL: among leads naming the birthplace, the share whose extracted chain reaches the birthplace's first Wikidata ancestor (parent) and the share reaching its Wikidata country.
- E4 SILENCE: when the text gives no containment for the birthplace, the result is a typed gap. Reported: the share of such leads, and of those, how many gold has a parent for (not recoverable from this text).
- E5 vs a regex: baseline B_regex = after "born in", the first capitalised phrase is the birthplace and the capitalised phrase after the next comma is its parent. The read-based extraction must beat it on E3 or the read's graph adds nothing for this.
- E6 THE JUDGE ON AN EXTRACTED NEST: on the same items as round 28 (city, country, where with an asker) restricted to people whose nest the text recovered to at least two holons, the share of verdicts equal to those of the judge over the Wikidata nest. Reported with the baselines' agreement.
- E7 FALSIFIER for the cues: if precision (E2) is not above that of a random-parent null (each child assigned a random other place-like being of the same text) the cues carry nothing.

## What this does and does not show
It shows how much of the nesting the read can compute on its own from one paragraph, scored by an independent source. It does not show nesting for places the text never relates, nor class nouns ("which county") beyond position; those stay typed gaps. The leads are short, so recall will be modest by construction.

## ADDENDUM 1 (2026-11-19, before any held-out text was read or scored)
Found on the development split, using the lead paragraphs: only 67 of 437 leads name the person's birthplace at all (15%), so the held-out would have scored about 55 people and about 15 steps. The text is therefore changed from the lead to the FIRST 5,000 CHARACTERS of the English Wikipedia article (the lead plus the early-life section where it exists), one article per request (the API returns several extracts only for the intro); the development subset is the first 100 even-hash people, the held-out is every odd-hash person. The lead-only development numbers are kept as a record of this finding, not as development of the extractor.
Other declared deviations: (a) each text is read through the same canonical door with `quality: false` (no clause parse), because the extractor uses only `cast` and `edges`, which the quality stage does not change, and the full read took about a minute per text; (b) texts shorter than 200 characters are skipped by the reader and are counted in the report; (c) the scorer matches labels by case-folded string, so a true historical container that Wikidata's chain omits ("West Germany" for Bremen) counts as a wrong step; the report lists the wrong steps so this can be read.
From the same development lead reads, three more things noted and NOT repaired before the held-out run (they are results, not bugs to fit): the extractor treats any being that sits after a comma beside a place as a place, so "Minneapolis, Minnesota, Prince" gives Minnesota a parent "Prince", and "Berlin, Hamburger Kammerspiele" a theatre as the parent; the cast needs a being to recur, so a place named once is not a cast being (the lead-only reach was 55 named in cast of 67 whose text names it); the lead-only dev precision was 10 of 17 steps (0.59) against a random-parent null of 0.21, with recall of the gold parent 1 of 48.

## ADDENDUM 2 (2026-11-19, before any held-out text was read or scored)
1. REFERENTS, NOT STRINGS (your correction). Nesting is between referents: a node is a cast being, every surface the read gave it is an alias of the one referent, and an answer string is resolved to its referent before the verdict is read off the referents' path (`nestFromRead` now carries each being's surfaces; E6 resolves the answer through `refOfLabel`, not string equality). The scorer bridges an extracted referent to the Wikidata node by any of the referent's surfaces, walking up the gold path so that a city and its same-named province (Lima, Lima) are told apart by position. The regex baseline (E5) stays a string method on purpose. The development numbers were rerun after this change: steps 12 of 18 (0.667), null 0.208.
2. TEXT. The article fetch (first 5,000 characters, one page per request) was throttled by Wikipedia (HTTP 429) to about 40 articles in 17 minutes and was stopped; it cannot supply the held-out in reasonable time. The held-out is therefore scored on the LEAD paragraphs already fetched, as originally preregistered, and the expected low reach (about 15% of people have a lead that names the birthplace) is the result to report, not repaired. The article fetch script is kept for a later round.
3. One more reported number, not a claim: recall of ANY gold ancestor (the first-parent recall of E3 is capped because Wikidata's parent is often a county the text never names).
