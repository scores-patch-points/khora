## 2. Where they agree

1. **The table's grouping of grammar into cells carries no detectable information beyond arbitrary grouping.** Measured two ways on disjoint data: PUD translation-invariance is at the null (0.764 against 0.776), and the table's clause cell agrees with the raters at 5.0% against 28.0% for always saying the modal cell. This is agreement in direction, not one test confirming the other: they ask different questions.
2. **Clause rank does not predict grain.** Mine, on instances: kappa −0.0135. Theirs, on sentences: odds ratio 1.04 (0.78 to 1.34), Ground 6.2% with a subordinate clause against 6.3% without (`RESULTS.md` H5).
3. **Syntax carries more than the obligatory morphology the table names.** Mine: of the four PUD channels only relations sit above their null (0.785 against 0.767, p 0.069, not significant); features (0.655 against 0.673) and parts of speech (0.806 against 0.840) sit below. Theirs: morphology alone has mean AUC 0.516, with relations and word classes added 0.602, and relations plus word classes beat morphology in 20 of 22 languages.
4. **The imperative is not a Ground thing.** Mine: Mood=Imp is 5.6% adjunct (607 instances), though the table gives it a Ground cell. Theirs: Mood=Imp into NUL·Ground is significantly negative in both raters (−0.590, −1.114).
5. **The case placements are the weakest.** Mine: nominative 0.07, gender 0.12, accusative 0.24, genitive 0.32 (Pearson r across languages, features only). Theirs: Case=Gen −0.943, Person=2 −1.353 and VerbForm=Part −0.729 are significantly contradicted; nominative ranks 22 of 27.
6. **The Ground half cannot be tested well on what exists.** Ground is 6.2% of the consensus labels, nine cells with 6 to 86 each. In mine, four cells (NUL·Figure, INS·Ground, INS·Pattern, REC·Ground) have no English tokens in PUD, so the comparison cannot be read there.

## 4. What survives

- **A unit's own embedding recovers a verb's operator, weakly** (0.329 leave-family-out, 0.403 on fresh words), as a proposer: plain accuracy is below always answering INS, and junk strings are pulled toward DEF. It is wired as a proposer with those caveats. It speaks for a verb's lexical class through an LLM's English glosses. It does not show grammatical categories sit in the same cells across languages, and the PUD result says they do not. The falsification session's H4 ("cells classify moves, never content") sits beside it as not killed, not supported.
- **The ablation idea does not survive**, at word, phrase or clause: the bare unit beats it every time.
- **Negation is a real, replicable category** whatever its cell: Polarity=Neg replicates in 19 of 23 languages in their data; my list of free-standing negators from UD gold covers 169 of 245 languages, recall 0.844 and precision 0.983 for the dominant forms (73 languages with a held-out check); 76 languages have none annotated, either because negation sits inside the verb or because the treebank does not mark it, and that absence is not evidence about the language. A lexicon, not a hypothesis test.
- **A small use for code**: neighbour back-off for unseen name heads, JavaScript only, overall AUC +0.01.
- **The two raters agree most on Figure cells and least on Ground**: kappa 0.469 CON·Figure, 0.446 INS·Figure, 0.407 EVA·Figure, against 0.11 to 0.29 for every Ground cell. A rival 18-cluster model beats the 18 cells by 4.4 nats, which the falsification session says it would, as it optimises that criterion; so the 18 are not earned as a distinct layer by that test.
