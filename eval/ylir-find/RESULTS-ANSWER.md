Causes confirmed by experiment:
1. The passage filter (the anchor's rarest word) dropped the sentences that state the facts on the subject's own page ("Dante was born in Florence." read alone gives Florence, but the word "alighieri" is not in it; "He was born in Frankfurt" has no name at all). Handing the whole page over alone made the result WORSE (5 to 3).
2. Because the decision rule treats every candidate as a rival and declines when two disagree, FALSE CANDIDATES block correct answers, so more evidence meant more conflicts. The false candidates came from the hunt's own reading, each a precision defect: dates that are not dates ("two", "at the age of 79", "14", the page number 221); a pronoun taken for the page's subject when it is in a subordinate clause ("until he died in 1751"), has the wrong gender ("She died when Dante was ten") or follows a sentence about someone else ("Rudolf was his student. But he died in 1902"); a surname inside another person's name ("Christiane von Goethe died in 1816"); German title words read as the English verb ("Die falsche Spitzfindigkeit" is a verb "die" in a bibliography line); places said in a row ("in Königsberg in the Kingdom of Prussia": the first answers); a place named for the person ("Villa Wittgenstein"); two sentences run together by the extraction ("25 August.Concurring reports").
3. All earlier arms ran on the stand-in priors home, where "Aristotle" and "Goethe" are not names.
Still gaps (18): "Who wrote X?" (7): the author stands next to the work as a lead (Goethe, Wittgenstein, Kant are the leads) because authorship is said as a possessive ("Kant's Critique of Pure Reason"), which no tier reads; the article for the work or its author is not retrieved for 6 questions (Shakespeare birth, World War I, War and Peace, the Divine Comedy, Hamlet: common-word anchors prefer long documents); Nietzsche, Goethe, Wittgenstein birth place: the text says "Born on 15 October 1844, … grew up in Röcken", where the birth place is not under "born". Wrong (2): Wittgenstein's death "May 1902" (a brother, said with a bare "he"), Plato's teacher "Sophist" (an introduction by Jowett).
